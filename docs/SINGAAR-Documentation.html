1. What SINGAAR is

SINGAAR is a marketplace where independent women artists and small salons list their services and prices, and customers (brides, bridesmaids and families) find, compare and book them. It covers weddings and the muhurtham, parties, hair, skin, nails, mehendi and accessories.

It is a marketplace, not a company with its own staff. It connects customers with artists and holds no inventory. The name means adornment, the act of getting ready. The brand idea is rituals: the sindoor, the mehendi evening, the nikah, the moment before the muhurtham.

2. The problem

Small salons and independent women artists in Bangalore and Chennai lose bookings and regular customers because there is no simple way to be found, compared and booked. Today it runs on Instagram messages, WhatsApp haggling and phone calls.

WhoWhat goes wrong today
Brides and familiesThey cannot judge a fair price, so a lower price can look like lower quality. They cannot be sure the artist will arrive on time on the wedding morning. They compare by scrolling Instagram and asking relatives.
Independent artists and small salonsNo way to be discovered beyond word of mouth. Dates are tracked in notebooks and chats. They lose bridal bookings to big parlours with bigger marketing.

This came from home. My mother has run a beauty parlour for about fifteen years. Her customers only phone her, and she loses bridal bookings to big parlours.

Primary

Conversations and videos with salon owners and artists, and my mother's own experience of how customers book.

Full write-up in the repository (docs/research).

  • Closest competitors: WedMeGood (a bridal directory with starting prices) and Fresha (a salon booking marketplace with an India presence). Urban Company and YesMadam are at-home services with an assigned professional, a different model.

5. The product: customer and artist journeys

A. Customer

  1. Opens SINGAAR and sees the logo and one search bar. Chooses a look and a city, then taps Get started. If she is unsure, a four-tap "Find my look" quiz helps.
  2. Compares artists: photos, a price guide for the city, who is free on her date, and who comes to her venue.
  3. Opens a profile and sees her work and the price of the service she searched for (all prices behind one tap). Checks her date and sends a booking request.
  4. The artist confirms. The customer can pay a small advance to lock the date (test checkout in the prototype).
  5. In Bookings she opens the booking chat, types a message, and gets a reply.
  6. Track arrival starts. For a home visit the artist moves towards her; for a salon visit the route runs from her to the salon; for the muhurtham a schedule counts back from the wedding time.
  7. After the service she leaves a review.
  8. Planning tools: wedding countdown with calendar reminders, a ritual-by-ritual plan (Hindu, nikah, Christian, party), and the bridal party planner.

B. Artist or salon

  1. Taps I am an artist. Sees the artist page, then Sign in (a demo sign-in; phone verification comes at launch) or List yourself (name, first service, price, optional WhatsApp and Instagram). Sign-in lasts only for that visit, and the customer and artist spaces are completely separate.
  2. Requests: a card with the date, customer, service, price and place, and big Accept or Decline buttons.
  3. Calendar: blocks dates. Accepted dates show as booked in customer search.
  4. Prices: edits services and prices, which show on her profile at once.
  5. Earnings: what she earned, the SINGAAR commission, what she keeps, and the platform fee.
  6. Chat with the customer from a confirmed booking.

6. How the booking works, step by step

StepCustomer seesArtist seesSystem state
1Sends a request for a service and dateA new request card with a date chiprequested
2"Waiting for her reply"Accept or Declinerequested
3"Confirmed", chat opens, pay advanceDate blocked in her calendar and in searchconfirmed
4Advance paid (test), date locked"Advance received"confirmed, paid
5Track arrival: map, ETA, timelineMark completed after the serviceconfirmed
6Leaves a reviewEarnings updated, commission calculatedcompleted

Other states: declined and cancelled. In the demo, a sample artist confirms by herself after about 35 seconds so a presenter can show the whole flow; a real artist taps Accept.

Rules built into the flow

  • Advance: 20% of the price, at least ₹500, rounded to the nearest ₹100. The balance is paid after the service.
  • No double booking: a date that is booked or blocked cannot be accepted again.
  • Tracking by service: bridal (the muhurtham) counts back from the wedding time (artist leaves about 5 hours before, arrives about 4 hours before, ready 30 minutes before). Other services start straight away, like a ride, with the artist 45 minutes out.

8. How AI was used

Building the product

The prototype was designed, coded, tested and revised with Claude Code (Anthropic's AI coding assistant), working from my brand, research and mentor feedback. I directed the product, made every decision about what stays and what goes, and rejected redesigns I did not like. Each change was tested in a real browser at phone and desktop sizes, with automated checks of every journey before it went live.

In the product

The class web app (web/ folder) uses the Claude API for two jobs:

  1. Understand: turn a bride's free-text sentence ("bridal makeup near Indiranagar under 20k") into a structured search: city, look, budget, style.
  2. Draft: write the artist's reply to a booking request.

Everything else is plain code: filtering, budget, date checks and ranking. A price check blocks any drafted message that contains a rupee amount that is not on the artist's own price list, so the AI cannot invent a price.

On the main prototype site the booking chat replies, the "describe your look" box and the artist's automatic confirmation are simple pre-written demo logic, not AI. The site says so. The full product would use the same Claude calls and the same price check.

Photos are free stock photography (Pexels), labelled "illustrative". They are not the sample artists, and are replaced by artists' own work at launch.

9. Business model and unit economics

We want to be far cheaper than the big platforms, so artists keep most of what they earn. Pricing is proposed and will be finalised together with the first artists.

Revenue lineHow it works
Commission10% of each completed booking. If she is not paid, we earn nothing.
Platform fee₹299 a month for the profile, calendar, bookings and tracking. Free for the first 3 months.

10. Trust, honesty, privacy and security

  • Honest labelling. The site says "sample", "preview" and "test checkout" wherever something is simulated. We do not show fake artists as real, fake reviews as real, or fake payments as real.
  • Privacy. The prototype collects no personal data. Bookings stay in the visitor's own browser. The booking form asks only for a first name, venue and an optional note.
  • Security. A strict Content Security Policy, no inline scripts, escaped user text, forms that cannot post to other sites, and no keys or secrets in the repository.
  • Accessibility. Skip link, labelled controls, keyboard focus handling, and reduced-motion support.
  • The SINGAAR promise (a standard we are building, not a working verification system yet): real work, a fair price, a trial look first, and we stand behind it. Reviews will only come from completed bookings.

11. Testing and quality

  • Every change was run in a real browser at phone size (375 wide) and desktop size before it was published.
  • Automated checks walk the main journeys: search, profile, booking request, artist accept, advance payment, tracking, calendar, prices, earnings, language switch, tour, direct links, and a check that no browser errors appear.
  • Regressions found and fixed along the way include dead buttons caused by a naming clash, an unreadable map on phones, stale cached files, and a tracker that followed the wrong artist.
  • Both live links were checked after each release to confirm the new version was being served.

15. Success measures

  • Understanding: a new visitor knows what to do on the home screen within five seconds.
  • Supply: number of real artists who list themselves and keep their calendar updated.
  • Demand: booking requests per artist per month, and the share that are accepted.
  • Trust: share of bookings completed and reviewed, and repeat bookings.
  • Money: completed booking value, commission earned, and artists still active after the free months.
